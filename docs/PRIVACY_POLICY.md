# Privacy Policy — DuBose Photography app

_Last updated: October 6, 2026. Publish this page on dubosephotography.com and link it from both app stores._

DuBose Photography ("we", "the studio"), 332 E Bay St, Charleston, SC 29401, operates the DuBose Photography mobile app. This policy explains what the app collects and how it is used.

## What we collect

- **Reservation requests**: parent name, email, phone, children's first names and ages, and any notes you enter when requesting a session time.
- **Hostess applications**: name, email, phone, city, state, and the details you provide.
- **Client sign-in**: your email address. We send a one-time code to it; we never store a password.
- **Proof activity**: which proofs you mark as favorites and the print orders you submit.
- **Photographs**: the proofs in your gallery were taken by the studio at your session and are shown only to the email address on file for that session.

The app does not collect location, contacts, advertising identifiers, or analytics, and does not show ads.

## How we use it

To schedule and confirm sessions, respond to hostess applications, deliver proofs, fulfil print orders, and answer your questions. We do not sell or share personal information with third parties, except service providers that store data for us (Supabase, which hosts our database and image storage in the United States, and our email provider for sending sign-in codes).

## Children

The app is for parents and guardians. We do not knowingly collect information directly from children under 13. Children's names and ages are provided by a parent for scheduling only.

## Retention and your choices

Galleries and orders are kept while your account is active so you can reorder prints. Email ordersdbp@gmail.com or call (843) 442-4096 to correct or delete your information, or to close your account; we will remove it within 30 days unless we must keep a record of a completed order.

## Security

Access to proofs is restricted to the signed-in client whose email matches the gallery, enforced by database security rules, and images are served through short-lived private links.
